Internet Speed Test

Is your connection as fast as your plan promises? Press start and this test measures three things in about twenty seconds: latency (ping) and jitter, how stable that latency is; download speed in Mbps; and upload speed in Mbps. The test runs between your browser and Cloudflare’s global network — the same measurement endpoints used by well-known open-source speed tools — so it works the same way for visitors in China and abroad, and no account or install is needed.

Press “Start speed test” to measure latency, download and upload. The test takes about twenty seconds.

Measured against Cloudflare’s global network (speed.cloudflare.com). Results depend on your local network and the distance to the node; the upload stage sends browser-generated random data, never your files.

How to use the Internet Speed Test

  1. Press “Start speed test” and wait — latency is measured first, then download, then upload.
  2. Read the three headline numbers: latency (with jitter), download Mbps and upload Mbps.
  3. Press “Run again” to retest, for example after moving closer to your router or switching networks.

Formula and method

Latency is the round-trip time of ten tiny requests to speed.cloudflare.com (the first, which pays the connection setup cost, is discarded); jitter is the average change between successive round trips. Download pulls staged files of growing size (100 KB up to 50 MB) from Cloudflare’s __down endpoint for up to about 10 seconds; upload posts browser-generated random data to the __up endpoint in growing sizes for up to about 10 seconds. Speed in Mbps = total bits transferred ÷ total seconds × 1,000,000 — totals, not an average of per-file speeds, so long transfers weigh correctly.

Example

On a healthy home fibre connection you might see latency around 10–40 ms with jitter under 5 ms, and download/upload speeds close to your plan’s headline rate when no one else is using the network. Use the “Load example” button above to try it with the sample inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my result lower than my plan’s speed?

The test measures the path from your device to a Cloudflare node, so Wi-Fi signal, router load, other devices, VPNs and the distance to the node all count. Test over a wired connection, close to the router, with other downloads paused, for the fairest reading.

How much data does one test use?

It depends on your speed: the staged downloads and uploads stop after about 10 seconds each, so a fast connection may transfer a few hundred megabytes in total, while a slow one transfers far less. Avoid repeated tests on a capped mobile plan.

What data is uploaded during the upload test?

Only random bytes generated in your browser (crypto.getRandomValues) — never your files. Cloudflare discards the payload; it exists only to be timed.

Why do results change between runs?

Networks are shared and conditions vary minute to minute. Run the test two or three times at different times of day and compare the pattern rather than a single number.
