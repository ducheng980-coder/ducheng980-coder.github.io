Tile Calculator

Buying floor tiles is easy to get wrong: order too few and the top-up batch may not match in colour; order too many and returns cost money. Enter the room size (or the laying area directly), pick the tile size and laying pattern, and this calculator works out the net tiles, the wastage allowance for your pattern, how many boxes to buy, and how many spare tiles to keep.

How to use the Tile Calculator

  1. Enter the room length and width in metres — or enter the laying area in m² directly.
  2. Pick the tile size (or “Custom size” and enter the tile length and width in mm) and the laying pattern.
  3. Check the wastage percentage (the pattern default is filled in; change it if your tiler quotes a different figure), enter tiles per box and spares, then calculate.

Formula and method

Tiles per m² = 1 ÷ (tile length × tile width in metres). Net tiles = ceil(area ÷ tile area). Tiles with wastage = ceil(area ÷ tile area × (1 + wastage)). Boxes = ceil((tiles with wastage + spares) ÷ tiles per box). Wastage defaults are the midpoints of the usual trade ranges: straight lay 5.5%, brick / running bond 6.5%, diagonal 45° and herringbone 11%, fishbone 13.5%.

Example

A 4 m × 3 m room with 800 × 800 mm tiles, straight lay: 20 tiles with wastage, 9 boxes of 3 (27 tiles) including 5 spares. Use the “Load example” button above to try it with the sample inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the laying pattern change the wastage?

Straight and brick layouts cut mostly at the edges, so 5–7% is usually enough. Diagonal, herringbone and fishbone layouts cut tiles at angles, which wastes more — typically 10–15%. The tool fills in the midpoint of the usual range for the pattern you pick; your tiler’s own figure wins if it differs.

Why keep spare tiles?

Tiles from a later production batch can differ slightly in shade, and a single broken tile is hard to replace years later. Keeping 5–10 whole spares from the same batch is the usual trade practice.

My room is not a rectangle — what do I enter?

Measure the net laying area (total floor area minus fixed fittings such as a bath or built-in cupboard) and enter it in the “Floor area” field, which overrides length × width. Add a little extra wastage for L-shaped rooms with many cuts.
