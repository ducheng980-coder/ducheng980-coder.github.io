HEIC to JPG Converter

iPhone photos save as HEIC by default, and many websites, forms and older apps still only accept JPG. This converter turns your HEIC and HEIF photos into standard JPGs entirely in your browser: nothing is uploaded to a server. How to use the HEIC to JPG Converter

  1. Choose or drop one or more HEIC or HEIF photos.
  2. Set the JPG quality (92 is a good balance of size and detail).
  3. Select “Convert all”, then download each JPG — or download everything at once as a ZIP.

Formula and method

HEIC photos are decoded in your browser with a WebAssembly build of libheif, the open-source HEIF decoder, then re-encoded as JPG with your browser’s built-in encoder. Decoding honours the photo’s orientation, so portrait shots stay portrait. Re-encoding strips the original HEIC metadata (including location); the JPG keeps the image itself at the quality you chose.

Frequently asked questions

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Decoding and conversion happen entirely in your browser with WebAssembly. You can verify it by loading this page and then switching off your network — the tool still works once the page is loaded.

Why do iPhone photos use HEIC?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) stores the same photo in about half the space of JPG, which is why Apple made it the iPhone default. The trade-off is compatibility: many websites, Windows apps and older devices cannot open it, so converting to JPG is the safe choice for sharing.

Will converting lose quality?

JPG is a lossy format, so a converted photo is not bit-for-bit identical to the HEIC original. At quality 92 the difference is very hard to see; lower the quality if you want smaller files, raise it towards 100 if you want to keep every detail.

What if a HEIC file contains several photos (burst / Live Photo)?

Some HEIC files hold more than one image. The tool converts every image inside the file and names them photo-1.jpg, photo-2.jpg and so on, so nothing is silently dropped.
