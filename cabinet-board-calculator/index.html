Cabinet Board Calculator

Ordering boards for fitted cabinets is easy to get wrong: the projection area on the quote is far smaller than the expanded board area, and the wrong wastage figure means a top-up order in a different batch. Enter each cabinet’s projection area (or the expanded area directly), pick the board type, and this calculator applies the usual expansion factors, adds the trade wastage for that board, and works out how many standard 1220 × 2440 mm sheets to buy — main boards and back boards counted separately.

How to use the Cabinet Board Calculator

  1. Enter the projection area (width × height, in m²) for each cabinet type you are building — or enter the main-board expanded area directly, which overrides the cabinet list.
  2. Pick the board type (the wastage default follows the usual range for that board; change it if your factory quotes a different figure) and, if needed, the back-board expanded area.
  3. Optionally enter the price per sheet for a cost estimate, then calculate.

Formula and method

Expanded area = projection area × expansion factor (wardrobe 2.2, bookcase 3.0, shoe cabinet 2.5, sideboard 2.2, other cabinets 2.2 by default). Area with wastage = expanded area × (1 + wastage). Sheets = ceil(area with wastage ÷ 2.9768), where 2.9768 m² is one standard 1220 × 2440 mm sheet. Wastage defaults are the midpoints of the usual trade ranges: particleboard 10%, multilayer board 12.5%, MDF 9%, imported board 12.5%.

Example

A wardrobe with 5 m² projection area and a bookcase with 3 m²: 20 m² expanded area, 22 m² with 10% wastage for particleboard — 8 sheets. Use the “Load example” button above to try it with the sample inputs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between projection area and expanded area?

Projection area is just the cabinet front (width × height). Expanded area unfolds every panel — sides, top, bottom, shelves and back — so it is much larger: a wardrobe runs about 2.2× its projection area, a shelf-heavy bookcase about 3×. Quotes priced by projection area already fold an expansion factor into the unit price; board ordering must use the expanded area.

Why are main boards and back boards counted separately?

Cabinet bodies normally use 18 mm boards while back panels use thinner 9 mm boards. They are different products at different prices, so the cutting list keeps them apart — mixing them would over-order the expensive 18 mm stock.

Why does the wastage rate differ by board type?

Cutting losses come from edge trimming and offcuts, and grain direction matters: multilayer boards lose more to grain matching (10–15%), particleboard typically 8–12%, MDF 8–10%. The tool fills in the midpoint of the usual range for the board you pick; your factory’s own cutting-list figure wins if it differs.
