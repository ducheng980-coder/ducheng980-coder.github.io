Cabinet Board Calculator

Ordering boards for fitted cabinets is easy to get wrong: the projection area on the quote is far smaller than the expanded board area, and the wrong wastage figure means a top-up order in a different batch. Two ways to count: in factor mode, enter each cabinet’s projection area (or the expanded area directly) and the calculator applies the usual expansion factors; in detail mode, enter one cabinet’s outside width, height and depth plus its dividers, shelves, drawers and doors, and every panel — sides, top and bottom, shelves, drawers, doors and the back — is counted piece by piece. Either way it adds the trade wastage for the board type and works out how many standard 1220 × 2440 mm sheets to buy, main boards and back boards counted separately.

How to use the Cabinet Board Calculator

  1. Choose the calculation mode: “Estimate by expansion factor” for a quick quote-stage count, or “Panel-by-panel from dimensions” to count one cabinet piece by piece.
  2. Factor mode: enter the projection area (width × height, in m²) for each cabinet type — or the main-board expanded area directly, which overrides the cabinet list — and, if needed, the back-board expanded area. Detail mode: enter the cabinet width, height and depth in mm, the counts of upright dividers, shelves and drawers, and how door panels should be counted; the back panel is worked out automatically as width × height.
  3. Pick the board type (the wastage default follows the usual range for that board; change it if your factory quotes a different figure), optionally enter the price per sheet, then calculate.

Formula and method

Factor mode: expanded area = projection area × expansion factor (wardrobe 2.2, bookcase 3.0, shoe cabinet 2.5, sideboard 2.2, other cabinets 2.2 by default). Detail mode (W, H, D = cabinet width, height, depth): side panels = (2 + upright dividers) × D × H; top & bottom = 2 × W × D; shelves = shelf count × W × D; each drawer = front W × front height + 2 sides D × 0.15 m + back W × 0.15 m + bottom W × D (drawer bottoms counted as 18 mm stock, which is conservative); doors = W × H (or a manually entered area, or excluded); back panel = W × H in 9 mm. Both modes: area with wastage = area × (1 + wastage); sheets = ceil(area with wastage ÷ 2.9768), where 2.9768 m² is one standard 1220 × 2440 mm sheet. Wastage defaults are the midpoints of the usual trade ranges: particleboard 10%, multilayer board 12.5%, MDF 9%, imported board 12.5%.

Example

Detail mode: a 2000 × 2400 × 600 mm wardrobe with 1 upright divider, 3 shelves and 2 drawers (240 mm fronts), particleboard — main panels 19.44 m² (sides 4.32 + top & bottom 2.4 + shelves 3.6 + drawers 4.32 + doors 4.8), 8 main-board sheets with 10% wastage; back panel 4.8 m², 2 sheets. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between projection area and expanded area?

Projection area is just the cabinet front (width × height). Expanded area unfolds every panel — sides, top, bottom, shelves and back — so it is much larger: a wardrobe runs about 2.2× its projection area, a shelf-heavy bookcase about 3×. Quotes priced by projection area already fold an expansion factor into the unit price; board ordering must use the expanded area.

Why are main boards and back boards counted separately?

Cabinet bodies normally use 18 mm boards while back panels use thinner 9 mm boards. They are different products at different prices, so the cutting list keeps them apart — mixing them would over-order the expensive 18 mm stock.

Why does the wastage rate differ by board type?

Cutting losses come from edge trimming and offcuts, and grain direction matters: multilayer boards lose more to grain matching (10–15%), particleboard typically 8–12%, MDF 8–10%. The tool fills in the midpoint of the usual range for the board you pick; your factory’s own cutting-list figure wins if it differs.

What is the difference between detail mode and factor mode?

Factor mode multiplies the projection area by a trade expansion factor — fast, and good enough at quote stage, but the factor is an average: a cabinet packed with dividers and drawers uses more board than the factor suggests, a plain one less. Detail mode counts one cabinet from its outside dimensions, panel by panel — sides, top and bottom, shelves, each drawer box, doors and the back — so the count follows the actual construction. It works from gross outside dimensions without deducting board thickness or edge banding, so treat it as a material estimate and confirm the exact sheet count against the factory cutting list before ordering.
