Ecommerce Profit Calculator

A listing that sells well can still lose money once platform fees, shipping, ad spend, shipping insurance and returns are counted. Enter the buyer-paid price, product cost, shipping and packaging, pick the platform (its usual fee rate is filled in — change it to your own category rate), add the average ad cost per order, shipping insurance and your return rate with the cost of one return, and this calculator works out the net profit on one order, the net margin, the break-even ROAS for your ad spend, and the lowest price that still breaks even — plus the price you need for any target profit per order.

Financial figures on this page are estimates for planning only and are not financial advice.

How to use the Ecommerce Profit Calculator

  1. Pick the platform — the usual fee rate is filled in automatically; change it to the rate your seller backend shows for your category.
  2. Enter the buyer-paid price (after any seller-funded coupons or discounts), product cost, and shipping plus packaging per order.
  3. Add the average ad cost per order, shipping insurance per order, return rate and the cost of one return; optionally enter a target profit per order, then calculate.

Formula and method

Net profit per order = buyer-paid price − product cost − shipping & packaging − platform fee (paid price × fee rate) − ad cost per order − shipping insurance − expected return loss (return rate × cost per return). Net margin = net profit ÷ paid price. Break-even ROAS = paid price ÷ profit before ads. Break-even price = (product cost + shipping + ad cost + insurance + return loss) ÷ (1 − platform fee rate).

Example

A ¥100 order on a Taobao C-store (0.6% fee) with ¥40 product cost, ¥5 shipping, ¥10 ad cost, ¥1 shipping insurance and a 10% return rate at ¥8 per return: net profit ¥42.6, margin 42.6%, break-even ROAS 1.9, break-even price ¥57.14. Frequently asked questions

Which price do I enter — the list price or the price after discounts?

The price the buyer actually pays: list price minus any seller-funded coupons, store discounts or platform promotions you pay for. Platform fees are charged on that paid price, so entering the list price would overstate both the fee and the profit.

What do the platform presets actually cover?

They are reference values, not a promise: Taobao C-store 0.6% is the payment-service fee (category technical service fees are separate), Tmall 3.5% is the midpoint of the usual 2–5% category range, Pinduoduo 0.6% is the standard transaction service fee (activity service fees of 1–3% are separate), and Douyin Shop 3% is the midpoint of the usual 2–5% range. Check your seller backend for your category and overwrite the rate — the field stays editable.

Why is the return loss a “per-order” figure, not the cost of one return?

A returned order costs return shipping, rework and sometimes an unsellable item, but only a share of orders are returned. The tool spreads that cost across all orders: return rate × cost per return. A 20% return rate at ¥15 per return costs ¥3 on every order, including the ones that stay sold.

What is the break-even ROAS telling me?

It is the lowest blended ROAS your ads can run at without losing money on the order: paid price ÷ profit before ads. If your real blended ROAS is below it, each paid order loses money even though the listing looks profitable before ads. The figure uses profit before ads, so it already accounts for product cost, fees, shipping and returns.

Is the net profit the shop’s final profit?

No — it is the contribution of one order after all per-order variable costs. Fixed costs (rent, staff, software subscriptions) still come out of it: net profit per order × monthly effective orders − monthly fixed costs is the shop’s pre-tax profit.
